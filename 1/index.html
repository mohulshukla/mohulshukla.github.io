Project 1: Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii Collection

Background

Sergei Mikhailovich Prokudin-Gorskii photographed the Russian Empire in the early 1900s by taking three exposures of the same scene through blue, green, and red filters on a single glass plate. The Library of Congress later digitized those plates as tall grayscale images: the top third is blue, the middle is green, and the bottom is red. This project automatically extracts those three channels, aligns green and red to blue, and stacks them into an RGB photograph.

Approach

To start I split each plate into B, G, and R by height. Before splitting, I cropped 10% off the left and right of the full scan (those borders are shared). After splitting, I crop 10% off the top and bottom of each channel (each exposure has its own edge artifacts). The alignment is a search over translations (dy, dx), where dy is a vertical shift (rows) and dx is a horizontal shift (columns). Each candidate shift is applied with np.roll. After shifting, I crop a thin interior of size max_shift + 2 before scoring so wrap-around pixels from np.roll do not dominate the metric. The pre-crop removes the original plate borders, and the per-shift crop only handles invalid pixels created by rolling.

The matching score I used is Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC). I followed the spec for its implementation, wherein both patches are converted to float, mean-subtracted, then scored as a normalized dot product. I used NCC instead of L2 because the three filter exposures are not the same brightness, so a raw pixel difference can prefer a bad shift. Mean-subtracting and normalizing makes the score care about structure more than overall intensity.

For the small JPEGs, a single-scale search over about ±15 pixels is enough. For the large TIFFs, displacements can be much larger and thus be time-intensive, so I use a recursive image pyramid: downsample by 2, estimate the shift on the smaller image, multiply that shift by 2, then refine locally (±2 pixels) at the current resolution. When the image is already small (min(h, w) < 300), the pyramid falls back to the single-scale search.

Multi-scale pyramid results (TIFFs)

Captions list (dy, dx) for green→blue and red→blue.

The emir portrait still looks slightly off. I suspect this is because the three channels do not have similar brightness, so NCC on raw intensities can lock onto a false peak (with a 5% pre-crop, red went to (94, −210)). With a 10% pre-crop the red shift became (75, 42), which is much more plausible, and the portrait is largely aligned. Although as can be seen some color fringing still remains.
